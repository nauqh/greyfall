# Kingsrest

The canon for this land. Where it and anything else disagree, this wins; the rules and the world's story are in [../reach.md](../reach.md).

## The land

The old kings' moor. They kept the Old Law, dig no deeper than a grave, and
were buried exactly that deep, as near the Hearth's warmth as the law allowed,
so they would keep warm till the world's end. They have. Kingsrest's dead are
not restless; they are warm, and after dusk they walk their own roads, the
Mourners' Way and the Barrow Path, as they did when they were carried down
them. Kingsrest folk step aside for them and do not think it strange.

The barrow-wardens, the masks, are older than the Order. They keep the Old
Law, wear the old kings' faces carved in bone, and hex anyone who digs. The
grave-robbers at the Diggings dig anyway, for the kings' gold, which is warm.

When the Citadel went grey, its road west came over the moor, and Kingsrest
barred it at the Grey Gate. It buried its key, the Horned Key, with the
eldest of the wardens in the King's Barrow, where nobody would dig. He has not
lain down since. Highward rode out across the moor against the Grey with forty
spears; they lie somewhere under Barrow Field, and only Corwin came back. The
Grey Road still runs east from the gate past the Burned Hamlet, where the
taken walked first.

- **Kingsrest.** The old kings' moor. Barrows, the warm dead on their own
  roads after dusk, the Diggings, Corwin's forty under Barrow Field, the
  Troll's Grave, and the Barrow King in the King's Barrow on the Horned Key.
  The Grey Gate is its east edge; the Barrow Cave goes down further than the
  moor has names for.

## For writers only

- The dead here are not taken. Warmth keeps them; the Grey is cold. They
  are the Old Law kept, not broken, and the barrows carve it.
- The Barrow King is a living man, the eldest mask, though nobody on the
  moor has seen his face under the bone in a lifetime.
