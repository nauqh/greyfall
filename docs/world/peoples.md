# The peoples of Aurum

Who else lives in Aurum besides men, how they stood before the Cold Year, and
why they are on the roads now. The world's story is in [reach.md](reach.md);
this file follows its rules and [GLOSSARY.md](../../GLOSSARY.md).

## The heart of it

**They are not monsters. They are cold.**

Under the old kings every people kept its own country, and the Old Law kept
them apart as surely as it kept the gold in the ground: men in the middle
lands, the others at the edges, in the fens, the crags, the sea, the deep
places. Each people had found its own warmth and lived by it.

The Cold Year took that warmth from all of them at once. Since then every
people has been moving toward what warmth is left, and into one another's
way. That is why the roads are full, why the wood is crowded, why goblins
fight men for a fire. Each people answers the cold in its own way, and each
answer is one of the answers men gave too: keep it, take it, wear it, let it
go out.

Men in Aurum do not have a word for all of them together. Lanternport says
"the other folk" when it is being polite, and nothing when it is not.

## The peoples

### Gnomes

The tinkers. Small, quick, and the cleverest hands in Aurum with a winch, a
lift, a pulley or a cannon. They learned before anyone that warm gold rises,
and they built Sunhold. The ones who went up with the gold became the
gnome-kings and were not seen below again. The ones who stayed kept the lifts
and the Sky Bridge running and were paid in the leavings.

Since the Cut no gold goes up and no pay comes down. Brasscrag is a rock full
of gnomes who built a city in the sky, own none of it, and have every cannon
on every rim pointed at whoever comes up the road. Their young carry
slingshots before they carry tools.

They count everything, and they remember every debt. Ask a gnome what Sunhold
owes them and they will tell you to the coin.

### Goblins

The diggers. Goblins fit where men do not, and the Citadel paid them in coal
and fire to work its deepest shafts. They dug the Deep Cut. When the cold came
up it, they were the first to know and the first to run, up the shafts with
their torches lit, and they never went down again.

They went where it was warmest: Emberwood. They keep their torches burning
day and night, burn what they cannot carry, and ride the Citadel's old pit
pigs, which came up the Cut with them. Men call them fire-mad. A goblin would
say a fire is the only thing that has never lied to them.

Men blame them for the Cut, because they dug it. Goblins blame men, because
men paid for it. Both are right.

### Gnolls

The dogs. Gnolls had no country before the Cold Year; they followed herds,
armies and plagues, and ate what was left. Now they follow the Grey. They
run at its edge, where the taken are slow and the living are tired, and pick
over whatever it leaves.

The Grey does not seem to take them. Nobody knows why. The Order says it is
because there is nothing in a gnoll warm enough to want.

### Lizard-folk

The fenfolk, and the oldest people in Aurum. They were in the fen before the
old kings, and the kings left them to it. Their blood runs with the weather:
warm, they are quick; cold, they sleep. They laid their eggs in the warm
springs that fed the fen from below.

The Cold Year chilled the springs and slowed the fenfolk to a crawl. The
Stilled came, built their chapel over the sinkhole, and took the eggs into it
"to keep them". The fenfolk guard the chapel stair now, and pour water into
the sinkhole with the sisters, because the eggs are inside.

Ask a fenfolk why it serves the Stilled and it will not answer. It may not be
able to.

### Trolls

The sleepers. Trolls are big, slow and half stone, and they have always slept
the cold months away in caves, as stone, waking when it warms. Old men in
Kingsrest say the Grey's last stage, when the taken stop and go to stone, is
the Grey doing to men what trolls do by nature.

Since the Cold Year most trolls have not woken. The few that are awake sit
where the water runs warm and will not be moved. The Cinder Troll sits in the
outflow at Tidewake, where the Hearth's water comes up into the sea cave,
and keeps the fishers' boat from it.

### The horned

The bullmen. A herding people of the high pastures east of Kingsrest, who
swore themselves to the Citadel's lords a long age ago and became its guard:
champions, gatekeepers, the horned men on the walls. A bullman's oath is to a
house, not a man, and outlives both.

When the Citadel went grey its bullmen went grey with it, at their posts. They
are the strongest of the taken, and still keep the oath. The ones in
Graveroad's pen were the Citadel's fighting champions, and still fight.

One did not stay. The horned watched over the Citadel's pits while goblins
dug them, and when the cold came up the Cut one bullman left his post and
walked west for the warm. He lies in the Ember Hollow, the hot ash grown into
his hide, and the goblins, who obeyed the horn for a hundred years, call him
lord: the Ember Bull. He is the one horned the Grey never had, unless the
hero came ashore as one.

Folk do not say "the horned" kindly. Highward's lords used to hire
them.

### Bamboo-folk

The hats. A quiet people from over the sea who came with the gold trade, when
ships still put in, and stayed. They grow bamboo on Brasscrag's high terraces,
where the gnomes' furnaces keep the ground warm, and sell it to the gnomes for
scaffolds and lift-poles.

They have not gone cold, because the groves are warm, and they want nothing
from anybody. They fight only when someone cuts their bamboo, or comes at
their own. Their grandsire, Old Bamboo, walked the wheel that winds the
gnomes' lift, up on the Tinker Peak, for bamboo. The Grey came over the Sky
Bridge and took him at it. He sits on the peak and eats from habit, and his
cubs stand round him and will not leave.

### Finfolk

The sharks. A sea people. They hunt the deep channels, trade with the pirates
of the far cove, and do not come ashore farther than a harpoon throws. The
Grey has never come down to the salt, so the finfolk are the one people in
Aurum the cold has not touched, and they care least of anyone what happens on
land.

They know one thing men do not. The warm water that comes up at Tidewake
carries things, and they have pulled more than one of the drowned out of it
alive. They have a word for those. They do not share it.

### Imps

The sparks. Imps are what gold breeds when it is left too long in one place:
small, hot, quick things that live off the warmth in gold the way moths live
off a lamp. There were always a few in any treasury.

Now they are wherever gold is left unguarded: the wreck of the last barge on
Cloudstair, the Throne Isle, the edges of Sunhold. Sunhold's guard spends half
its days burning them out. The other half, folk say, it spends wondering why
there are more every year.

## As the hero

The Hearth gives back the drowned of any people, not only men. Three of the
other folk can come ashore as the hero, drawn from their own sheets washed
blue, the band's colour:

- **The Eggwarden**, a fenfolk: it went down the Stillmere sinkhole after the
  eggs and followed the water to the Hearth. Its frill cuts every foe beside
  it at once.
- **The Champion**, one of the horned: sworn to the Citadel, it rode out with
  the last gold ship the night the Grey came, and went down with it. The
  Ember Bull is not alone, then, in being horned and never the Grey's. Every
  blow it lands throws the foe back.
- **The Grovekeeper**, a bamboo-folk: when the Grey took Old Bamboo it took
  ship for the old country to bring back help, and went down with the ship.
  The most health, and it guards behind its nunchaku.

The folk's lines do not yet change for a hero who is not a man.

## Not peoples

Men do not count these as folk, and neither should player text.

- **Beasts:** bears, giant bats, bumblebees, bomb fish, snakes, spiders,
  turtles, sheep. They want warmth like anything, and are cruder about it.
  The Weaver and the Shell Below are beasts grown old and large on the
  Hearth's warmth; neither talks.
- **The dead:** the skulls of Kingsrest and Graveroad are the old kings'
  dead, warm in their graves and walking because of it (see the Old Law).
- **Men:** thieves, robbers and pirates are men, in their own colours.
- **The masks:** the hex shamans are men too. They are the barrow-wardens of
  Kingsrest, an order older than the Order of the Hearth, who keep the Old
  Law, wear the old kings' faces carved in bone, and hex anyone who digs. The
  Barrow King is the eldest of them.

## Open questions

1. **Can any of them join the band?** The band is Lancer, Archer, Monk, all
   men in blue. A fenfolk, one of the horned or a bamboo-folk can now be the hero, washed blue (see
   As the hero); the same wash would serve a companion.
2. **Do any of them talk to the wanderer?** Today no folk who is not a man
   speaks. One gnome trader in Brasscrag, or a fenfolk at the chapel stair,
   would carry most of this file in two talks.
3. **What is the finfolk word for the drowned who come back?** Keep it
   unsaid, or let it be the one thing a finfolk ever tells the wanderer?
